# Embraso language

## Purpose and language

Combine software freedom with human connection: useful software, community, and gradual digital autonomy across Brazil's different realities. Write with warmth, clarity, and conviction, without pitch language, guilt about not participating, or invented promises.

These skill instructions and other implementation documentation are in English. The editorial collection, including internal story and strategy documents, remains in Brazilian Portuguese. Produce Brazilian Portuguese copy unless explicitly requested otherwise; preserve Portuguese quotations, examples, canonical names, and file paths.

## Method

1. Identify the audience, surface, intent, and whether the information is a principle, intention, fact, account, or hypothesis. When no material user decision is required, follow the document conventions.
2. Choose the appropriate reference. Do not put the entire story, organizational plan, or licensing discussion in the manifesto. Preserve the remaining material in the corresponding internal editorial document; do not erase perspectives because they do not fit a publication.
3. Write one strong idea, a concrete explanation, and, when appropriate, an invitation. Proposed signature: “Tecnologia livre. Gente por perto.” Treat it as an option, not a requirement for every piece. Make the initiative's stage clear.
4. Separate usable copy from editorial notes. Do not automatically publish whole files, internal material, or historical sources. “Internal” is not access control: do not add secrets or personal data without authorization.
5. Review accuracy, warmth, and consistency. Deliver the text, its location, and only the unresolved issues that prevent publication; do not turn every sentence into a public disclaimer.

## Invariants

- Preserve the six names and their order when presenting the manifesto: Liberdade por padrão; Soberania por escolha; Segurança por princípio; Pragmatismo com propósito; Tecnologia para o bem comum; Transparência e liberdade algorítmica.
- Use Brazilian Portuguese for editorial output; “queremos” and “defendemos” express intention and position. Use “temos”, “oferecemos”, and “já fazemos” only with confirmation. Do not speak for a community as if consensus had been recorded.
- Free software is not necessarily free of charge; source visibility is insufficient; commercial use is compatible with software freedom. Do not choose a license, legal status, funding model, or private-development policy through wording alone.
- Autonomy is not isolation. Proprietary tools, cloud services, and AI can be conscious choices, with costs, risks, and continuity options. Security and privacy are not absolute guarantees.
- Community includes people who do not code. Knowledge of the problem and maintenance have value; collaboration does not presume unpaid work, an available position, or unlimited availability.
- Political criticism can be firm. Do not turn predictions, accusations, third-party metrics, or personal recollections from the sources into established facts. Preserve context and disagreements internally rather than neutralizing them for convenience.
- Do not invent products, a team, a CTO, numbers, partnerships, email addresses, channels, deadlines, or policies. A submission CTA requires a real destination; a success message requires a confirmed action.
- A literal quotation preserves its wording and source. A rewrite is an adaptation; a newly written phrase is a new proposal. Do not attribute Embraso-specific commitments to external authors.
- Do not build the website, install a stack, or create flows at this stage. The indicated visual reference was not successfully rendered during the initial documentation work; do not describe its layout by assumption.

## Before finishing

Check the text against the manifesto and voice guide. If the direction changed, update affected documents; if you added story, an idea, or a disagreement, keep it referencable in the internal editorial collection. Verify local links and the conditions supporting claims. Do not edit historical copies to fit the narrative.
